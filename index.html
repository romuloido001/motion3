Understanding Motion

Grade 7 Science · Distance and displacement, speed and velocity, graphing motion, and variables. About 60 minutes.

Who is learning today?

Goals: explain distance vs. displacement, tell speed from velocity, read distance-time graphs, and name the variables in an investigation.

1. Distance vs. displacement

Distance is the total length of the path (scalar). Displacement is the straight-line change in position from start to finish, with direction (vector).

Try it. Home is at 0. East is to the right. Walk out to the park, then walk back toward home.

Distance = 8 km   Displacement = 4 km east

Notice: when you return to where you started, distance keeps growing but displacement becomes 0.

Practice

2. Speed vs. velocity

Speed = distance ÷ time (scalar). Velocity = displacement ÷ time (vector, so direction matters).

Worked example. A car travels 120 km in 2 hours. Speed = 120 ÷ 2 = 60 km/h. If it drove east the whole way, its velocity is 60 km/h east.
Worked example 2. A cyclist rides 3 km west, then 3 km east in 30 minutes total. Speed = 6 km ÷ 0.5 h = 12 km/h. Displacement = 0, so average velocity = 0.

3. Graphing motion

On a distance-time graph, a straight sloped line means uniform velocity. A steeper line means faster. A flat line means not moving. A curve that bends up means speeding up.

Practice

4. Identifying and controlling variables

Independent: what you change. Dependent: what you measure. Controlled: what you keep the same so the test is fair.

Example. Ramp height (10, 20, 30 cm) changes a toy car's speed. Independent: ramp height. Dependent: speed (stopwatch). Controlled: same car, same surface, same starting point.
